Teaching & mentoring

From a clear question to independent scientific judgment

Mentoring is one of the most meaningful parts of my work. I have been fortunate to support 15+ undergraduate and junior researchers at Harvard and MIT through quantitative rigor, encouragement, and progressively greater research ownership.

Mentoring approach

Three habits I hope to cultivate

01 Frame

Together, turn a broad idea into a measurable question with meaningful controls.

02 Troubleshoot

Treat unexpected results with curiosity by documenting decisions, failure modes, and evidence before choosing the next experiment.

03 Own

Build the confidence to interpret results, communicate uncertainty, and develop an independent direction.

Selected outcomes

Grateful for mentees’ growth and achievements

Prize-winning thesis

I am especially proud that Lauren Levy's senior thesis on hydrogel stabilization of mRNA–LNPs received the MIT Bioengineering Merck Prize and the Koch Institute Marcella O'Grady Boveri Medal.

It has been rewarding to see undergraduate mentees Lauren Levy and Chloe Tan become first authors on a peer-reviewed review of microneedle-based mRNA vaccine delivery.

Progressive ownership

I am grateful to have watched mentees progress from experimental training to independent analysis, presentation, authorship, and a best-poster award.
